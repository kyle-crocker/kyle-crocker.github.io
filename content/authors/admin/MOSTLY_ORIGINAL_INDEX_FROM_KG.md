Microbial communities are complex and consequential metabolic factories, performing biochemistry that drives the global cycling of nutrients throughout the biosphere, and underlies interactions with humans, plants, and other hosts. Microbes carry out their critical work in the context of diverse ecological communities. Much like a factory production line, microbial communities divide the burden of metabolic “labor” over many species that take on different roles. Unlike the production line, however, the organization of microbial communities emerges from a complex interplay between ecological and evolutionary processes. 

My research program is about discovering **how nature builds its metabolic factories**, i.e., learning how constraints on metabolism and physiology give rise to the microbial communities we see in nature.
